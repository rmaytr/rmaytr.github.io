Undergraduate Researcher — Computational Plasma Physics
Dr. Renato Camata Research Group · UAB Department of Physics · Birmingham, AL
May 2026 — Present

The Camata group studies pulsed-laser growth of thin-film materials, with an emphasis on Fe-based superconductors and related metal chalcogenides, combining experiments with machine learning, AI-enhanced process control, and plasma simulation. I am one of 3 students on the computational and simulation team in a 12-student lab composed mostly of graduate students.

  • Develop and run coupled laser-ablation and fluid-dynamics simulations of laser-generated plasmas in C using the Basilisk PDE framework
  • Run simulation workloads on UAB’s Cheaha supercomputer; learned Linux and Bash from scratch to manage HPC jobs, environments, and data on the cluster
  • Generate synthetic plasma datasets in which plasma temperature (T) and ionization fraction (Xe) serve as ground truth for validating machine-learning models
  • Build Python pipelines that process, visualize, and analyze simulation output, turning raw data into the plots and comparisons the team uses to evaluate models
  • Support research on machine-learning-guided pulsed laser deposition (PLD), in which Gaussian Process Bayesian Optimization builds surrogate models of plasma conditions from a small number of experiments
  • Co-author of the abstract “Machine Learning-Assisted Pulsed Laser Deposition Based on Plasma Optical Emission Signatures,” submitted to the AVS 72nd International Symposium with co-authors from UAB Physics and the Center for Nanophase Materials Sciences at Oak Ridge National Laboratory
  • Across 1,000 independent optimization trials, the model found optimal plasma conditions in as few as three adaptive iterations — a step toward autonomous, real-time control of thin-film growth
